# Design Direction — Tensile Architectural Structures

## Three Initial Approaches

### Theme Name: Desert Modernism
**Very Brief Intro:** Warm stone, sunlit fabric and architectural shadows create a premium regional identity that feels grounded, calm and crafted. The site reads like an architecture studio rather than a construction catalogue.
**Probability:** 0.071

### Theme Name: Coastal Structure Lab
**Very Brief Intro:** Airy blue-green tones, bright daylight and fluid curved compositions express tensile membranes as lightweight, elegant architecture. The mood is optimistic, technical and spacious.
**Probability:** 0.028

### Theme Name: Monsoon Blueprint
**Very Brief Intro:** A restrained editorial system combines ink, paper, blueprint lines and saturated fabric color to communicate engineering confidence without becoming a dark industrial template. The mood is intelligent, precise and distinctive.
**Probability:** 0.083

## Chosen Approach: Desert Modernism

### Design Movement
Contemporary regional modernism blended with architectural editorial design: warm mineral surfaces, deep blue shadow, bold photographic crops, and restrained technical annotations.

### Core Principles
1. **Architecture first:** Completed structures and their spatial effect lead the page; text supports the visual story.
2. **Warmth over harsh contrast:** Replace black/orange construction clichés with limestone, ivory, eucalyptus and ink-blue tones.
3. **Asymmetric editorial rhythm:** Use offset image crops, split rails, oversized numerals and staggered project cards rather than a repetitive centered grid.
4. **Proof in context:** Every claim is paired with process evidence, project facts, material detail or a clear next action.

### Color Philosophy
The base is sun-washed ivory and pale limestone, referencing fabric under daylight and architectural concrete. Ink blue carries trust and depth without the severity of black. Eucalyptus green gives a calm material accent; terracotta is reserved for tiny highlights that echo warm structures, never used as a dominant orange theme.

### Layout Paradigm
A left-aligned editorial canvas with a persistent slim project index, full-bleed image moments, offset content blocks and occasional dark “blueprint” bands. Sections should feel like pages of an architecture monograph, not stacked marketing cards.

### Signature Elements
- A thin vertical **project index rail** with section numbers and short labels.
- Fine blueprint contour lines and measurement ticks used as quiet background texture.
- Large image crops with small caption labels such as “Lahore / Cantilever / 2025”.

### Interaction Philosophy
Interactions should feel like exploring a portfolio: clicking a project expands its facts, hovering reveals a location and system type, filters rearrange projects without a page reload, and the quote panel opens as a calm project brief rather than an aggressive pop-up.

### Animation
Use 180–260ms ease-out transitions for buttons, nav and cards. Hero image receives a slow 10-second scale drift only when motion is allowed. Project cards reveal captions on hover; process steps draw a thin line as they enter. Respect reduced-motion preferences. No bouncing, neon glow, or noisy scroll effects.

### Typography System
Use **DM Sans** for body and UI, paired with **Cormorant Garamond** italic for occasional architectural editorial emphasis. H1 is 64–78px desktop and 42px mobile; body is 16–18px with generous line-height. Small labels are uppercase with 0.16em tracking.

### Brand Essence
**Positioning:** Custom tensile shade and membrane architecture for clients who want beautiful spaces engineered for real conditions.  
**Personality:** Assured, thoughtful, exacting.

### Brand Voice
Headlines are concise and visual. CTAs are direct but calm. Microcopy explains what happens next instead of shouting urgency.

Example lines:
> “Shade becomes architecture when every curve has a reason.”

> “Tell us about the space. We’ll bring the structure into focus.”

### Wordmark & Logo
Use a simple abstract mark made from two intersecting tension curves forming an offset architectural “T” silhouette, paired with a custom-spaced wordmark. The mark should work alone in the header and favicon; do not use a typed default logo.

### Signature Brand Color
**Eucalyptus Teal — #2D6A68.** It is ownable, calm and materially connected to outdoor shade without looking like a generic corporate blue or a warning orange.

## Style Decisions

- No black-and-orange construction-company palette.
- No generic centered hero with a text block floating over a dark gradient.
- Use a warm ivory canvas, ink-blue type, eucalyptus accent and limited terracotta detail.
- The homepage must visibly feature real or art-directed tensile architecture imagery.
- Every major section must include a visual anchor, interaction, or proof element.
- The design should feel editorial and architectural, not like a static brochure.
